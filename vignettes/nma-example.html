Ranking with MIDs: NMA example

Introduction

This vignette shows how to rank treatments in a standard network meta-analysis (NMA) while requiring a minimally important difference (MID) when ranking treatments. The approach follows Curteis et al. (2025), Ranking of treatments in network meta-analysis: incorporating minimally important differences.

The example uses the Senn2013 diabetes data supplied with multinma which was originally sourced from Senn 2013. The dataset consists of 26 studies on 10 treatments (including placebo), and the outcome under consideration is the mean difference in change from baseline in mean HbA1c, a measure of blood sugar attached to haemoglobin and an indicator of average blood sugar levels in preceding weeks.

Data and network

Each row displays mean differences (y) and associated standard errors (se) in HbA1c for a treatment (trt) compared to a reference treatment in each study (study).

Choosing the MID

The MID must be on the same scale as the model’s treatment effects. Here the effects are mean differences in HbA1c (percentage points), so we require a difference of at least 0.5 before one treatment is ranked above another.

mid <- 0.5

Rankings without an MID

For comparison, the standard multinma rankings:

rank_0 <- posterior_ranks(diabetes_fit_FE, sucra = TRUE)
plot(rank_0)

Rankings with an MID

rank_mid <- posterior_ranks_mid(
  diabetes_fit_FE,
  summary = TRUE,
  sucra = TRUE,
  mid = mid
)

Handling ties

When treatments are not separated by an MID they share a rank. The method argument controls how tied treatments are ranked:

  • "midpoint" (default, recommended): tied treatments receive the average of the ranks they span, consistent with Curteis et al. (2025).
  • "min": tied treatments receive the best (lowest) rank in the tie.
  • "max": tied treatments receive the worst (highest) rank in the tie.
rank_min <- posterior_ranks_mid(diabetes_fit_FE, mid = mid, method = "min")
rank_max <- posterior_ranks_mid(diabetes_fit_FE, mid = mid, method = "max")
